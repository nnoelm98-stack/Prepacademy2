Ratios & Proportions
Master ratio setup and cross-multiplication, direct and inverse variation, and the multi-step proportion problems the SAT uses to test algebraic reasoning.
13 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
Ratios, rates, and proportions
A ratio compares two quantities. A proportion states that two ratios are equal. Cross-multiplication is the fastest way to solve proportions.
a/b = c/d  →  ad = bc     (cross-multiply)
Works whenever two fractions are equal to each other
Direct variation
y = kx — as x increases, y increases proportionally. k is the constant of variation.
If y varies directly with x and y=12 when x=4:
k = 12/4 = 3 → y = 3x
When x=7: y=21
Inverse variation
y = k/x — as x increases, y decreases. The product xy is always constant (= k).
If y varies inversely with x and y=6 when x=4:
k = 6·4 = 24 → y = 24/x
When x=8: y=3
Worked example — part-to-part vs part-to-whole
A mixture has oil and water in ratio 2:5.
Total parts = 2+5 = 7
Fraction that is oil = 2/7
Fraction that is water = 5/7
If total = 350 mL → oil = (2/7)·350 = 100 mL
SAT strategy tips
Part-to-part vs part-to-whole
A ratio of 3:5 means 3 parts to 5 parts. Total parts = 8. Fraction of whole = 3/8 or 5/8. The SAT often gives a part-to-part ratio and asks for a fraction of the whole — add the parts first.
Scale problems: set up a proportion
For unit conversions, maps, or scaled drawings, always set up a proportion with matching units. If 1 inch = 40 miles, then 3.5 inches = x miles → 1/40 = 3.5/x → x = 140 miles.
Direct vs inverse: read the problem
"Varies directly" → y = kx → as x doubles, y doubles. "Varies inversely" → y = k/x → as x doubles, y halves. Find k from the given pair, then use it to find the unknown.
Check units throughout
In proportion problems, make sure corresponding quantities have the same units on each side. Miles/hour on the left must match miles/hour on the right — not miles on one side and hours on the other.
Practice questions
Problem
A recipe uses 3 cups of flour for every 2 cups of sugar. If a baker uses 15 cups of flour, how many cups of sugar are needed?
3/2 = 15/s
Set up a proportion: flour/sugar = flour/sugar. Cross-multiply: 3·s = 2·15. Solve for s.
Question 1 · Proportions
How many cups of sugar are needed?
A8
B10
C12
D9
Correct! 10 cups
3/2 = 15/s
3s = 30
s = 10 cups ✓
Not quite. Set up the proportion keeping flour over sugar on both sides: 3/2 = 15/s. Cross-multiply and solve for s.
3s = 30 → s = 10
Problem
The number of workers w and days d to complete a job are inversely proportional. 8 workers complete the job in 15 days.
w · d = k (constant)
Inverse variation: w·d = constant. Find k first using the given values: k = 8·15 = 120. Then use k to find d when w = 12.
Question 2 · Inverse Variation
How many days will 12 workers take to complete the same job?
A8 days
B10 days
C12 days
D18 days
Correct! 10 days
Inverse variation: w·d = k
k = 8·15 = 120
12·d = 120
d = 10 days ✓
Not quite. For inverse variation, the product is constant: w₁·d₁ = w₂·d₂. Use 8·15 to find k, then solve 12·d = k.
k=120 → d=120/12=10
Problem
In a class, the ratio of boys to girls is 3:4. There are 28 girls in the class.
boys/girls = 3/4
Set up a proportion: 3/4 = boys/28. Solve for boys. Then total students = boys + 28.
Question 3 · Ratio — Part to Whole
How many total students are in the class?
A49
B42
C35
D56
Correct! 49 students
3/4 = boys/28
4·boys = 3·28 = 84
boys = 21
Total = 21 + 28 = 49 ✓
Not quite. Use the ratio to find boys: 3/4 = boys/28. Cross-multiply to find boys. Then add boys + girls for total.
boys = 21 → total = 49
