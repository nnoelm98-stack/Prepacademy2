Percentages
Master all percentage calculations — percent of a number, percent change, reverse percentages, and successive percent changes — one of the most reliable point sources on the SAT.
12 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
The five percentage problem types
Percent = (Part / Whole) × 100
Part = Whole × (Percent / 100)
% Change = (New − Old) / Old × 100
Type 1 — Find the percent
What percent of 80 is 20?
20/80 × 100 = 25%
Type 2 — Find the part
What is 35% of 120?
120 × 0.35 = 42
Type 3 — Find the whole (reverse %)
18 is 30% of what number?
18 / 0.30 = 60
Type 4 — Percent change
Price went from $40 to $52. What % increase?
(52−40)/40 × 100 = 30%
Type 5 — Successive % changes
Two percent changes in sequence — multiply the multipliers, don't add the percentages.
+20% then −20%: 1.2 × 0.8 = 0.96 → net −4%
(NOT 0% as you might expect)
Type 6 — Percent of percent
A store marks up an item 40%, then discounts it 25%. Net effect?
1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05 → net +5%
Multiply the multipliers, read the result.
Worked example — reverse percentage
After a 15% discount, a jacket costs $85. What was the original price?

After 15% off: price = original × (1 − 0.15) = original × 0.85
So: 0.85 × original = 85
Original = 85 / 0.85 = $100

Common mistake: adding 15% to $85 gives $97.75 — that's wrong because 15% of the discounted price ≠ 15% of the original.
SAT strategy tips
Use multipliers for percent change
For any percent change, use a multiplier: +r% → multiply by (1 + r/100). −r% → multiply by (1 − r/100). This works for forward and reverse calculations, and chains easily for successive changes.
Reverse percentage — divide, don't subtract
If a price after a 20% increase is $120, the original is $120 ÷ 1.20 = $100 — not $120 − $24 = $96. Always divide by the multiplier to reverse a percentage.
Successive changes — multiply, don't add
Two 10% increases is NOT a 20% increase. It's 1.10 × 1.10 = 1.21, which is a 21% total increase. Never add percentage changes — always multiply their multipliers.
Percent greater/less than traps
"A is 25% greater than B" → A = 1.25B. "A is 25% less than B" → A = 0.75B. The SAT uses these phrasings carefully — "greater than" means the multiplier is above 1, "less than" means below 1.
Practice questions
Problem
A store increases a product's price by 20%, then later reduces the new price by 20%.
Use multipliers: +20% → ×1.20, then −20% → ×0.80. Multiply them together. The result shows the net multiplier relative to the original price.
Question 1 · Successive % Changes
What is the net percent change from the original price?
A0% — the price returns to the original
B+4% — the price is slightly higher
C−4% — the price is slightly lower
D−20% — the increases and decreases cancel
Correct! −4%
Net multiplier: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96
0.96 = 1 − 0.04 → net change = −4%

Example: $100 → ×1.20 → $120 → ×0.80 → $96
$96 is 4% less than the original $100. ✓

The percentages don't cancel because 20% of $120 ≠ 20% of $100.
Not quite. Don't add the percentages — multiply the multipliers: 1.20 × 0.80. Try with a concrete number: start with $100, apply each change, and see where you end up.
$100 × 1.20 = $120 · $120 × 0.80 = $96 → −4%
Problem
After receiving a 12% raise, an employee earns $3,136 per month.
After a 12% raise: new salary = original × 1.12. So original = new ÷ 1.12. Divide the new salary by the multiplier to reverse the percentage.
Question 2 · Reverse Percentage
What was the employee's salary before the raise?
A$2,759
B$2,800
C$2,950
D$3,512
Correct! $2,800
New = Old × 1.12
3,136 = Old × 1.12
Old = 3,136 / 1.12 = 2,800
Check: $2,800 × 1.12 = $3,136 ✓

Common error: $3,136 − 12% = $3,136 × 0.88 = $2,760 ✗
(That would reverse a 12% decrease, not increase.)
Not quite. To reverse a 12% increase, divide by 1.12 — don't subtract 12% from the new salary. Subtracting 12% undoes a decrease, not an increase.
3,136 ÷ 1.12 = $2,800
Problem
In a survey, 60% of respondents said they exercise regularly. Of those who exercise regularly, 45% said they exercise more than 5 times per week.
This is a "percent of a percent" problem. Find 45% of the 60% who exercise regularly. Multiply: 0.60 × 0.45 = what fraction of all respondents?
Question 3 · Percent of Percent
What percent of all respondents exercise more than 5 times per week?
A27%
B45%
C105%
D36%
Correct! 27%
60% exercise regularly
45% of those exercise 5+ times/week
Fraction of ALL: 0.60 × 0.45 = 0.27 = 27% ✓

Option B (45%) is the percentage among exercisers only — not all respondents.
Option D (36%) adds instead of multiplies: 60%+45%−something — wrong approach.
Not quite. 45% is the percentage within the subgroup — not the whole survey. To find what fraction of everyone exercises 5+ times, multiply: 60% of respondents × 45% of those.
0.60 × 0.45 = 0.27 = 27%
