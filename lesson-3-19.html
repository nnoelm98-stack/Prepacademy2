Unit Analysis & Conversions
Master dimensional analysis — the systematic method for converting units and solving multi-step rate problems by tracking units like algebraic variables until only the target unit remains.
11 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
Dimensional analysis — treating units as algebra
Every measurement has a number and a unit. In dimensional analysis, you multiply by conversion factors — fractions equal to 1 — that cancel unwanted units and introduce the target unit. Units cancel exactly like variables in algebra.
The conversion chain method
[Given value] × [conversion₁] × [conversion₂] × ... = [target value]
Set up each conversion factor so the unit you want to eliminate is on the bottom, and the unit you want to introduce is on top. Units cancel diagonally — if the same unit appears in a numerator and denominator, cross it out.
Worked example — multi-step conversion
A car travels at 60 miles per hour. Convert this speed to feet per second.
(Use: 1 mile = 5,280 feet; 1 hour = 3,600 seconds)

60 miles/hour × (5,280 feet / 1 mile) × (1 hour / 3,600 seconds)
= 60 × 5,280 / 3,600 feet/second
= 316,800 / 3,600 = 88 feet/second

Notice: "miles" cancelled (it appeared in numerator and denominator), "hours" cancelled — only "feet/second" remains.
Worked example — rate × rate
A factory produces 240 widgets per hour. Each widget requires 3 parts. How many parts are used in 5 hours?

5 hours × (240 widgets/hour) × (3 parts/widget)
= 5 × 240 × 3 = 3,600 parts

"Hours" and "widgets" both cancel — only "parts" remains. ✓
SAT strategy tips
Write units in every step
Never do unit conversion mentally. Write the full fraction with units at every step. Units cancel just like algebraic variables — if the same unit is on top and bottom, cross it out. This prevents the most common conversion errors.
The target unit must be the only one left
After cancelling all units in your chain, only the target unit should remain. If you still have unwanted units, you need another conversion factor. If you're missing the target unit, you set up a fraction upside down.
Rate × rate chains are powerful
For problems with multiple rates (widgets/hour and parts/widget), multiply them as a chain. The middle unit (widgets) cancels, leaving the final unit (parts/hour). This works for any chain of compatible rates.
Identify the target unit first
Before setting up any conversion, write down the unit you need at the end. Then build your chain backwards — each conversion factor introduces what the next step needs, until you have the target.
Practice questions
Problem
A swimming pool holds 45,000 liters of water. A pump drains the pool at a rate of 250 liters per minute.
Set up: 45,000 liters ÷ (250 liters/minute) = minutes. Then convert minutes to hours by dividing by 60.
Question 1 · Rate Conversion
How many hours will it take to drain the pool completely?
A3 hours
B18 hours
C180 hours
D30 hours
Correct! 3 hours
Time = 45,000 liters ÷ 250 liters/min = 180 minutes
Convert to hours: 180 min × (1 hr / 60 min) = 3 hours ✓
Not quite. First find the time in minutes: 45,000 ÷ 250. Then convert minutes to hours by dividing by 60.
45,000 ÷ 250 = 180 min · 180 ÷ 60 = 3 hours
Problem
A printer prints 40 pages per minute. Each page uses 0.5 grams of ink. Ink cartridges hold 120 grams each.
Chain the rates: pages/min × grams/page = grams/min. Then find how many minutes one cartridge lasts: 120 grams ÷ (grams/min).
Question 2 · Chained Rates
How many minutes does one ink cartridge last?
A3 minutes
B6 minutes
C60 minutes
D240 minutes
Correct! 6 minutes
Ink usage rate: 40 pages/min × 0.5 grams/page = 20 grams/min
Time per cartridge: 120 grams ÷ 20 grams/min = 6 minutes ✓

Unit check: (pages/min)(grams/page) = grams/min · grams ÷ (grams/min) = min ✓
Not quite. Chain the rates: 40 pages/min × 0.5 grams/page = 20 grams/min. Then divide the cartridge capacity by the rate.
120 grams ÷ 20 grams/min = 6 minutes
Problem
A scientist measures a concentration of 4.5 milligrams per deciliter (mg/dL). She needs to convert this to micrograms per milliliter (μg/mL).
Conversions: 1 mg = 1,000 μg · 1 dL = 100 mL
Set up the chain: 4.5 mg/dL × (1,000 μg/mg) × (1 dL/100 mL). Units should cancel to leave μg/mL.
Question 3 · Multi-Unit Conversion
What is 4.5 mg/dL expressed in μg/mL?
A0.045 μg/mL
B45 μg/mL
C450 μg/mL
D4,500 μg/mL
Correct! 45 μg/mL
4.5 mg/dL × (1,000 μg/mg) × (1 dL/100 mL)
= 4.5 × 1,000/100 μg/mL
= 4.5 × 10
= 45 μg/mL ✓

Unit check: (mg/dL)(μg/mg)(dL/mL) → mg cancels, dL cancels → μg/mL ✓
Not quite. Chain the conversions: 4.5 mg/dL × (1,000 μg/mg) × (1 dL/100 mL). The mg cancels and the dL cancels — what remains is μg/mL.
4.5 × 1,000 ÷ 100 = 45 μg/mL
