Statistics & Data Interpretation
Master mean, median, mode, range, and standard deviation — and learn how outliers and data changes affect each measure, the most tested aspect of statistics on the SAT.
13 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
The five key measures
Mean (average)
Sum of all values ÷ number of values. Affected by every data point — especially sensitive to outliers.
Data: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10
Mean = 30/5 = 6
Median
The middle value when data is ordered. For even-numbered datasets, average the two middle values. Resistant to outliers.
Data: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10 → median = 6
Data: 2, 4, 6, 8 → median = (4+6)/2 = 5
Mode
The value that appears most often. A dataset can have no mode, one mode, or multiple modes.
Data: 3, 5, 5, 7, 9 → mode = 5
Data: 1, 2, 3, 4 → no mode
Range
Maximum − Minimum. Measures the spread of the entire dataset. Extremely sensitive to outliers.
Data: 2, 4, 6, 8, 10
Range = 10 − 2 = 8
Standard deviation
Measures how spread out the data is around the mean. High SD = data is spread widely. Low SD = data clusters near the mean. You don't need to calculate it on the SAT — just interpret it: larger SD means more spread, smaller SD means more concentrated.
Dataset A: 10, 10, 10, 10 → SD = 0 (no spread)
Dataset B: 1, 5, 10, 15, 19 → SD is large (wide spread)
How changes to data affect each measure
The SAT's most common statistics question: what happens to mean, median, and range when you add, remove, or change a data value?
Add/remove a value equal to mean
Mean stays the same. Median may change. Range unchanged (if not extreme).
Add an outlier (very high or low)
Mean shifts toward outlier. Median barely changes. Range increases significantly.
Add a constant k to every value
Mean, median, mode all increase by k. Range and SD stay the same.
Multiply every value by k
Mean, median, mode all multiply by k. Range and SD also multiply by k.
Remove an outlier
Mean moves away from the outlier. Median may change slightly. Range decreases.
Replace a value with the mean
Mean stays the same. SD decreases (less spread). Range may decrease.
SAT strategy tips
Mean is the SAT's favorite statistic
Most statistics questions involve the mean. Remember: mean = sum ÷ count. To find a missing value when the mean is given: total sum = mean × count. Then subtract the known values to find the missing one.
Outliers move mean, not median
Adding or removing an extreme value changes the mean significantly but barely moves the median. If a question asks which measure is "most affected" by an outlier — the answer is always mean (and range).
Median requires ordering the data
Always sort the data before finding the median. For n values: if n is odd, median = value at position (n+1)/2. If n is even, median = average of values at positions n/2 and n/2 + 1.
SD interpretation without calculation
You never need to calculate SD on the SAT. Just know: higher SD = more spread from the mean. If two datasets have the same mean but different SDs, the one with lower SD has values clustered more tightly.

The scores of 5 students on a test are:
72859068x
The mean score is 82.
Mean = sum ÷ count. So sum = mean × count = 82 × 5 = 410. Add up the four known values and subtract from 410 to find x.

B82
C90
D75
Correct! x = 95
Total sum = 82 × 5 = 410
Known sum: 72+85+90+68 = 315
x = 410 − 315 = 95 ✓
Check: (72+85+90+68+95)/5 = 410/5 = 82 ✓

Problem
A dataset has 7 values:
479121518100
The value 100 is a data entry error. It is removed from the dataset.
Calculate the mean and median both before and after removing 100. Remember to re-sort and find the new middle value for the median of 6 values.
Question 2 · Effect of Removing an Outlier
Which statement correctly describes the effect of removing 100?
ABoth mean and median decrease significantly.
BMean decreases significantly; median changes only slightly.
CMean stays the same; median decreases significantly.
DBoth mean and median stay approximately the same.
Correct!
With 100: mean=(4+7+9+12+15+18+100)/7=165/7≈23.6 · median=12
Without 100: mean=(4+7+9+12+15+18)/6=65/6≈10.8 · median=(9+12)/2=10.5

Mean: 23.6→10.8 = large decrease ✓
Median: 12→10.5 = small change ✓
Outliers strongly affect mean but barely affect median.
Not quite. Calculate mean and median with and without 100. The outlier (100) pulls the mean far above most values. The median (middle value) is barely affected by extreme values at the ends.
Mean drops from ≈23.6 to ≈10.8 · Median changes from 12 to 10.5
Problem
Two classes took the same exam. Class A has a mean of 74 and standard deviation of 12. Class B has a mean of 74 and standard deviation of 4.
Both classes have the same mean — so the average performance is identical. The standard deviation tells you about spread. Lower SD = scores cluster tightly around the mean. Higher SD = scores are more spread out.
Question 3 · Interpreting Standard Deviation
Which statement best compares the two classes?
AClass A performed better on average than Class B.
BClass B's scores are more consistent — most students scored closer to 74.
CClass A's scores are more consistent — most students scored closer to 74.
DClass B had more students scoring above 74 than Class A.
Correct! Both classes have the same mean (74), so neither performed better on average — A is wrong. SD measures spread: Class B has SD = 4, meaning scores cluster tightly around 74. Class A has SD = 12, meaning scores are spread widely — more students scored far from 74. Option B correctly identifies Class B as more consistent. Option D cannot be concluded from SD alone.
Not quite. Both classes have the same mean — so average performance is identical. The difference is in standard deviation. Lower SD means scores are clustered closer to the mean (more consistent). Which class has the lower SD?
Class B: SD=4 → more consistent (tighter clustering around 74)
