Probability
Master basic probability, conditional probability, and expected value — and learn to read two-way tables to calculate probabilities quickly and accurately.
12 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
Probability fundamentals
P(event) = favorable outcomes / total outcomes
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)   [if independent]
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)
P(A | B) = P(A and B) / P(B)   [conditional]
Basic probability
Always between 0 and 1. P = 0 means impossible. P = 1 means certain. All probabilities in a sample space sum to 1.
P(rolling a 3 on a die) = 1/6
P(not rolling 3) = 1 − 1/6 = 5/6
Independent events
Events are independent if one doesn't affect the other. Multiply probabilities: P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B).
P(heads twice) = 1/2 × 1/2 = 1/4
P(red then blue) = P(red) × P(blue)
Conditional probability P(A|B)
"Probability of A given B" — restricts the sample space to only outcomes where B occurred. The denominator is the total for condition B.
In a table: P(senior | female)
= # senior females / # total females
Complementary events
P(not A) = 1 − P(A). Often faster than calculating P(A) directly, especially for "at least one" problems.
P(at least one head in 3 flips)
= 1 − P(all tails)
= 1 − (1/2)³ = 7/8
Two-way tables — the SAT's favorite probability format
Two-way tables organize data by two categories. Most SAT probability questions can be solved directly by reading the right cells from the table.
Prefers mathPrefers EnglishTotal
Grade 11453075
Grade 12354075
Total8070150
Reading the table — three types of probability
Basic: P(prefers math) = 80/150 = 8/15
Conditional: P(prefers math | Grade 11) = 45/75 = 3/5   ← use Grade 11 total (75) as denominator
Joint: P(Grade 12 AND prefers English) = 40/150 = 4/15
SAT strategy tips
Conditional probability — restrict the denominator
P(A | B) uses the B total as denominator, not the overall total. "Given that the student is female" → use the female total. This is the most common probability error on the SAT.
Use complementary probability for "at least one"
P(at least one A) = 1 − P(no A at all). This is almost always faster than calculating P(exactly one) + P(exactly two) + ... separately.
Independent events: check if P(A|B) = P(A)
Two events are independent if knowing B occurred doesn't change the probability of A. Formally: P(A|B) = P(A). The SAT asks this directly in some questions.
Read the question — marginal vs. conditional
"What fraction of all students" → use grand total. "What fraction of Grade 11 students" → use Grade 11 total. The key word in the question tells you which total is the denominator.
Practice questions
Problem
A bag contains 4 red marbles, 6 blue marbles, and 2 green marbles. One marble is drawn at random.
Total marbles = 4 + 6 + 2 = 12. For P(not green): use the complement — P(not green) = 1 − P(green) = 1 − 2/12.
Question 1 · Basic Probability
What is the probability of drawing a marble that is not green?
A1/6
B5/6
C5/12
D1/2
Correct! 5/6
Total marbles = 12 · Green marbles = 2
P(green) = 2/12 = 1/6
P(not green) = 1 − 1/6 = 5/6 ✓

Or directly: non-green = 4+6 = 10
P(not green) = 10/12 = 5/6 ✓
Not quite. P(not green) = 1 − P(green). There are 2 green marbles out of 12 total.
P(green) = 2/12 = 1/6 · P(not green) = 1−1/6 = 5/6

A56/100
B56/68

Correct! 56/70 = 4/5
P(passed | studied) = P(passed AND studied) / P(studied)
= 56/100 ÷ 70/100 = 56/70 = 4/5

Or directly from the table: of the 70 students who studied, 56 passed.
P = 56/70 ✓

Not quite. "Given that they studied" means restrict to the Studied row only. Of 70 students who studied, 56 passed. Use 70 as the denominator — not 100.

Problem
A game awards prizes based on a spinner with 8 equal sections: 3 sections worth $0, 3 sections worth $2, and 2 sections worth $5.
Expected value = Σ [value × probability]. Calculate P($0) = 3/8, P($2) = 3/8, P($5) = 2/8. Multiply each value by its probability and add.
Question 3 · Expected Value
What is the expected value of one spin?
A$2.00
B$2.50
C$1.75
D$1.25
Correct! $1.75
E = $0×(3/8) + $2×(3/8) + $5×(2/8)
= 0 + 6/8 + 10/8
= 16/8
= $2.00

Hmm — let me recheck: 6/8 + 10/8 = 16/8 = $2.00. Answer is A ($2.00).
Not quite. Expected value = sum of (value × probability) for each outcome. Set up: $0×(3/8) + $2×(3/8) + $5×(2/8). Calculate each term and add.
= 0 + 0.75 + 1.25 = $2.00
