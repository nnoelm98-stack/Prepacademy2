What changes in the calculator section
The concepts are the same as Module 3 — but the numbers are messier, the contexts are richer, and the problems are often multi-step. The calculator lets you handle arithmetic quickly so you can focus on the reasoning.

Worked example — real-world linear model
A plumber charges a $75 call-out fee plus $55 per hour. A competing plumber charges $45 per hour with a $95 call-out fee. After how many hours do they charge the same total?

Plumber 1: C₁ = 75 + 55h
Plumber 2: C₂ = 95 + 45h
Set equal: 75 + 55h = 95 + 45h
10h = 20 → h = 2 hours
Check: C₁(2) = 75+110 = $185 · C₂(2) = 95+90 = $185 ✓
SAT strategy tips
Let the calculator handle arithmetic, not reasoning
Set up the equation or system first — on paper, not in the calculator. Then use the calculator only to evaluate expressions or solve for exact values. Students who rely on guess-and-check lose time.
Check units in real-world problems
If the question gives rates in different time units (hours vs. minutes), convert first. Slope has units of (y-units per x-unit) — state them explicitly when interpreting a linear model in context.
Identify slope and intercept from word problems
The rate (per hour, per item, per mile) is always the slope. The fixed cost, starting value, or initial amount is always the y-intercept. Label both before writing the equation.
Two equations from two conditions
If a word problem gives two separate conditions, write one equation per condition. Then solve the system. This is the most reliable approach for any "two unknowns" word problem.

Problem
A line passes through the points (−3, 7.5) and (5, −0.5).
Slope = (−0.5 − 7.5) / (5 − (−3)) = −8/8 = −1. Then substitute one point to find b. Use calculator for verification.

m = −8/8 = −1 · b = 4.5 · y = −x + 4.5

Problem
The graph of line k has x-intercept at (6, 0) and y-intercept at (0, −4).
Two intercepts give you two points. Use them to find slope: m = (−4−0)/(0−6) = −4/−6 = 2/3. The y-intercept is b = −4, so the equation is y = (2/3)x − 4.
