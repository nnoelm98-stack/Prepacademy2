Probability & Expected Value
Go deeper on probability — multi-event problems, independent vs. dependent events, geometric probability, and expected value in complex real-world contexts.
13 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
Advanced probability concepts
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B|A)  [general]
P(A and B) = P(A) × P(B)  [if independent]
P(A or B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A and B)
E(X) = Σ xᵢ · P(xᵢ)  [expected value]
Independent events
Events A and B are independent if P(A|B) = P(A) — knowing B doesn't change A's probability. Multiply probabilities directly.
Two coin flips: P(HH) = ½ × ½ = ¼
Drawing with replacement = independent
Dependent events
Without replacement, the second event depends on the first. Use conditional probability: adjust the denominator after the first draw.
Draw 2 reds from 4R, 3B without replacement:
P = 4/7 × 3/6 = 12/42 = 2/7
Geometric probability
P(landing in region) = area of region / total area. Used when a random point is selected in a geometric figure.
Dart hits 8×8 board randomly. Circle of r=2 inside.
P(circle) = π(4)/64 = π/16 ≈ 0.196
Expected value
The long-run average outcome. Multiply each outcome by its probability and sum. A fair game has expected value = 0.
Win $5 (P=0.3), lose $2 (P=0.7):
E = 5(0.3) + (−2)(0.7) = 1.5−1.4 = $0.10
Worked example — dependent events without replacement
A bag has 5 red and 3 blue chips. Two are drawn without replacement. What is P(both red)?

P(first red) = 5/8
P(second red | first was red) = 4/7  (4 reds remain out of 7)
P(both red) = 5/8 × 4/7 = 20/56 = 5/14
SAT strategy tips
With replacement = independent; without = dependent
The phrase "without replacement" always means dependent events — adjust the denominator after each draw. "With replacement" means independent — the denominator stays the same every time.
Geometric probability: set up area ratio
For geometric probability problems, find the ratio of the favorable area to the total area. Express areas in terms of the given dimensions before simplifying.
Expected value can be negative
If expected value is negative, the game is unfavorable on average. The SAT may ask "how much should the ticket cost to make this a fair game?" — set E = 0 and solve for the cost.
"At least one" — use complementary probability
P(at least one event) = 1 − P(none at all). For "at least one success in n trials": 1 − P(all failures) = 1 − (1−p)ⁿ. This is always faster than summing individual cases.

A box contains 4 green, 3 yellow, and 2 purple balls. Two balls are drawn without replacement.
Without replacement = dependent events. P(first green) = 4/9. After drawing one green, 3 green balls remain out of 8 total. P(second green | first green) = 3/8.

What is the probability that both balls drawn are green?
A16/81
B1/6
C4/9
D2/9
Correct! 1/6
P(1st green) = 4/9
P(2nd green | 1st green) = 3/8
P(both green) = 4/9 × 3/8 = 12/72 = 1/6 ✓

Problem
A circular target of radius 6 is inscribed inside a square. A dart is thrown randomly and always hits the square.
The square has side = diameter = 12. Square area = 144. Circle area = π(6)² = 36π. P(circle) = 36π/144 = π/4.

Bπ/6 ≈ 0.524

Circle inscribed in square → side of square = 2r = 12
Area of square = 12² = 144
Area of circle = π(6)² = 36π
P(circle) = 36π/144 = π/4 ✓

This is a classic result: a circle inscribed in a square always has probability π/4.
