Ratios, Rates & Proportions
Apply ratios and proportions to complex multi-step problems — mixture problems, work rates, speed-distance-time, and scaling — the applied contexts the calculator section emphasizes.

Advanced ratio and rate applications
Mixture problems
Combine two substances with different concentrations/values. The total amount and total value must both be conserved. Set up two equations: quantity and value.
Mix 30% and 70% solution to get 50%:
0.30x + 0.70(100−x) = 0.50(100)
Solve for x.
Combined work rates
If A does a job in a hours and B does it in b hours, together they complete 1/a + 1/b of the job per hour. Time together = 1/(1/a + 1/b) = ab/(a+b).
A: 6 hr, B: 4 hr
Together: ab/(a+b) = 24/10 = 2.4 hours
Speed-distance-time
d = rt. Average speed = total distance / total time. For round trips where speeds differ, calculate each leg's time separately, then find total d and total t.
60 mph out, 40 mph back, 120 mi each way:
Time out = 2 hr, time back = 3 hr
Avg speed = 240/5 = 48 mph
Scaling with proportions
When all dimensions scale by factor k: perimeter/length scales by k, area scales by k², volume scales by k³. State which dimension changed and use the correct power.
Worked example — mixture problem
A chemist has a 20% acid solution and an 80% acid solution. How many liters of each should be mixed to make 60 liters of a 50% acid solution?

Let x = liters of 20% solution · (60 − x) = liters of 80% solution
0.20x + 0.80(60 − x) = 0.50(60)
0.20x + 48 − 0.80x = 30
−0.60x = −18 → x = 30 liters of 20% solution
30 liters of 20% + 30 liters of 80% → 60 liters of 50%
SAT strategy tips
Mixture: two equations — quantity and value
For every mixture problem, write one equation for total quantity (x + y = total) and one for total value (value₁·x + value₂·y = total value). Solving this system gives both amounts. Never try to guess — always set up both equations.
Work rates: add rates, not times
If two workers work simultaneously, add their rates (jobs/hour), not their times. Rate A + Rate B = Combined rate. Then time = 1/combined rate. Adding times together is a classic error.
Average speed ≠ average of two speeds
For a round trip or two-segment journey at different speeds, average speed = total distance / total time. Calculate each segment's time using d=rt, sum the times, then divide total distance by total time.
Multi-step rate chains: use unit cancellation
Chain rates as fractions so units cancel: (miles/hour) × (hours/day) = miles/day. Writing units explicitly prevents setting up the multiplication or division the wrong way.
Practice questions
Problem
Pipe A fills a tank in 4 hours. Pipe B fills the same tank in 6 hours. Both pipes are opened simultaneously.
Rate A = 1/4 tank/hr. Rate B = 1/6 tank/hr. Combined rate = 1/4 + 1/6. Find common denominator: 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 tank/hr. Time = 1 ÷ (5/12) = 12/5 hours.
Question 1 · Combined Work Rate
How long will it take to fill the tank with both pipes open?
A5 hours
B2 hours 24 minutes
C2 hours
D3 hours
Correct! 2 hours 24 minutes
Rate A + Rate B = 1/4 + 1/6 = 3/12 + 2/12 = 5/12 tank/hr
Time = 1/(5/12) = 12/5 = 2.4 hours
= 2 hours 24 minutes ✓

Note: 12/5 = 2.4 hr = 2 hr + 0.4×60 min = 2 hr 24 min
Not quite. Add the rates (not the times): 1/4 + 1/6 = 5/12 tanks per hour. Time to fill = 1 ÷ (5/12) = 12/5 hours.
12/5 = 2.4 hours = 2 hr 24 min
Problem
A car travels 120 miles to a destination at 60 mph, then returns the same 120 miles at 40 mph.
Calculate each leg's time separately: Time out = 120/60 = 2 hr. Time back = 120/40 = 3 hr. Total distance = 240 mi. Total time = 5 hr. Average speed = 240/5.
Question 2 · Average Speed
What is the average speed for the entire round trip?
A50 mph
B48 mph
C52 mph
D45 mph
Correct! 48 mph
Time out: 120/60 = 2 hours
Time back: 120/40 = 3 hours
Total time: 5 hours · Total distance: 240 miles
Average speed = 240/5 = 48 mph ✓

The average of 60 and 40 is 50 — but that's wrong here because you spend more TIME at the slower speed.
Not quite. Average speed = total distance ÷ total time. Find each leg's time separately: 120/60 and 120/40. Add times, divide total distance.
240 ÷ (2+3) = 240/5 = 48 mph
Problem
A coffee shop blends two types of coffee: Blend A costs $12/lb and Blend B costs $20/lb. The shop wants to create 50 lbs of a blend that costs $15/lb.
Let x = pounds of Blend A. Then (50−x) = pounds of Blend B. Value equation: 12x + 20(50−x) = 15(50). Solve for x.
Question 3 · Mixture Problem
How many pounds of Blend A should be used?
A25 pounds
B31.25 pounds
C18.75 pounds
D37.5 pounds
Correct! 31.25 pounds
12x + 20(50−x) = 15(50)
12x + 1000 − 20x = 750
−8x = −250
x = 31.25 lbs of Blend A ✓
Blend B: 50 − 31.25 = 18.75 lbs
Check: 12(31.25)+20(18.75) = 375+375 = 750 = 15×50 ✓
