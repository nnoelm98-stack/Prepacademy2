Percentages & Interest
Apply percentage skills to multi-step financial contexts — simple and compound interest, tax and tip problems, markups and discounts in sequence, and percent change across multiple periods.
12 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
Interest formulas and financial applications
Simple interest: I = Prt  ·  A = P(1 + rt)
Compound interest: A = P(1 + r/n)ⁿᵗ
Total cost with tax: C = price × (1 + tax rate)
Final price after markup then discount: P × (1+m) × (1−d)
Simple vs. compound interest
Simple: interest calculated only on principal. Compound: interest calculated on principal plus accumulated interest. Compound always yields more over time.
$1,000 at 5% for 3 yr:
Simple: I = 1000×0.05×3 = $150
Compound annually: 1000×(1.05)³ = $1,157.63
Sequential discounts and markups
Apply each multiplier in sequence — never add percentages. A 20% markup followed by a 20% discount is NOT 0% change: 1.20 × 0.80 = 0.96 → net −4%.
Item: $100 · +30% markup: $130
−15% discount: $130 × 0.85 = $110.50
Net change: +10.5%, not +15%
Tax, tip, and total cost
Add tax/tip percentage as a multiplier to the pre-tax price. To find original price given total including tax: divide total by (1 + tax rate).
Meal $45, 18% tip, 8% tax:
$45 × 1.18 = $53.10 (with tip)
$53.10 × 1.08 = $57.35 (with tax on tip+meal)
Finding original before percentage
After applying a percentage change, reverse it by dividing. After a 35% markup the price is $270: original = 270 ÷ 1.35 = $200. Never subtract the percent from the result.
SAT strategy tips
Compound interest: identify all 4 variables
Before computing A = P(1 + r/n)ⁿᵗ, explicitly label P (principal), r (annual rate as decimal), n (compoundings per year), and t (years). Misidentifying n is the most common error — monthly means n=12, quarterly means n=4.
Sequential changes: multiply, never add
For any sequence of percent changes, multiply the multipliers in order. Never add or subtract percentages. A 15% increase then a 10% decrease: 1.15 × 0.90 = 1.035 → net +3.5%.
Tax/tip order matters
If tip is calculated on the pre-tax amount and tax on the post-tip amount (or vice versa), the calculation differs. Read the problem carefully to identify what percentage applies to what base.
Simple interest: linear; compound: exponential
Simple interest grows linearly (straight line on a graph). Compound interest grows exponentially (curved). A = P(1+rt) is linear in t. A = P(1+r)ᵗ is exponential in t. The SAT tests which model applies.

Problem

After 10 years: A's amount = 4000(1 + 0.05×10) = 4000(1.5) = $6,000. B's amount = 4000(1.05)¹⁰. Use calculator for B.

Bapproximately $512
Capproximately $1,200
Dapproximately $257

Not quite. Calculate each separately. A: 4000×(1+0.05×10)=$6,000. B: 4000×(1.05)¹⁰ (use calculator). The difference shows the power of compounding over time.

Apply multipliers in sequence: markup → ×1.40, then discount → ×0.75. Net multiplier = 1.40 × 0.75. Compare to original wholesale cost (1.00).

B+5%
C−5%
D0% — the changes cancel out
Correct! +5%
Markup: × 1.40
Discount: × 0.75
Net: 1.40 × 0.75 = 1.05
= 5% above wholesale ✓

Option D is wrong because 40% and 25% don't cancel — 25% of $140 ≠ 40% of $100.

Problem
A restaurant bill totals $86.40, which already includes an 8% sales tax.
The $86.40 is the post-tax amount. Pre-tax = post-tax ÷ (1 + tax rate) = 86.40 ÷ 1.08. Don't subtract 8% of 86.40 — that gives the wrong answer.
