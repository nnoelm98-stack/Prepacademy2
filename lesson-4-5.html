Geometry Fundamentals
Master the area and perimeter formulas for all shapes on the SAT, angle relationships, and the coordinate geometry connection — all applied to real-world and multi-step problems.
13 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
Essential area & perimeter formulas
Rectangle
A = lw · P = 2l + 2w
l = length, w = width
Triangle
A = ½bh
b = base, h = perpendicular height (not slant side)
Circle
A = πr² · C = 2πr
r = radius. Diameter d = 2r.
Trapezoid
A = ½(b₁ + b₂)h
b₁ and b₂ = parallel bases, h = perpendicular height
Sector of a circle
A = (θ/360)πr²
θ = central angle in degrees. Arc length = (θ/360)·2πr
Composite shapes
A_total = sum of parts
Break complex shapes into rectangles, triangles, and semicircles. Add or subtract areas.
Angle relationships
Supplementary angles
Two angles on a straight line. Sum = 180°.
Vertical angles
Opposite angles at an intersection. Always equal.
Triangle angle sum
Three interior angles of any triangle sum to 180°.
Parallel lines cut by transversal
Alternate interior angles are equal. Co-interior angles sum to 180°.
Exterior angle of triangle
Equals the sum of the two non-adjacent interior angles.
Polygon interior angles
Sum = (n − 2) × 180° where n = number of sides.
Worked example — composite area
A window consists of a rectangle (6 ft wide, 4 ft tall) topped by a semicircle. Find the total area.

Rectangle area = 6 × 4 = 24 sq ft
Semicircle radius = 6/2 = 3 ft
Semicircle area = ½π(3)² = 4.5π ≈ 14.14 sq ft
Total = 24 + 4.5π ≈ 38.14 sq ft
SAT strategy tips
Label every measurement on the figure
Before computing, write all known measurements on the diagram. Mark right angles, equal sides, and parallel lines. This visual organization prevents missing a given value or using the wrong dimension.
Height ≠ slant side in triangles
In A = ½bh, h is the perpendicular height from base to opposite vertex — not the length of a slanted side. If only the slant side is given, use the Pythagorean theorem to find the height first.
Composite shapes: add or subtract
For complex shapes, identify which simpler shapes to combine. Sometimes it's easier to find the area of a large shape and subtract the "missing" pieces rather than adding small pieces.
Sector problems: always use the fraction θ/360
A sector is a "pizza slice" of a circle. Its area and arc length are both fractions of the full circle — multiply the full-circle formula by θ/360. This works for any angle, not just 90° or 180°.
Practice questions
Problem
A circular pizza has a diameter of 16 inches. It is cut into 8 equal slices.
Each slice is a sector with central angle 360°/8 = 45°. Radius = 8 inches. Use sector area formula: A = (45/360)πr².
Question 1 · Sector Area
What is the area of one slice? (Leave in terms of π.)
A4π square inches
B8π square inches
C2π square inches
D16π square inches
Correct! 8π square inches
Radius = 16/2 = 8 inches
Each slice: 360°/8 = 45°
Sector area = (45/360) × π × 8²
= (1/8) × 64π
= 8π square inches ✓
Not quite. Each of 8 equal slices has angle 45°. Sector area = (45/360) × π × r². With r = 8: (1/8) × 64π.
(1/8) × 64π = 8π sq in
Problem
In the figure, two parallel lines are cut by a transversal. One angle measures (3x + 15)° and the co-interior angle (same-side interior) measures (2x + 25)°.
Co-interior (same-side interior) angles between parallel lines are supplementary — they add up to 180°. Set up: (3x+15) + (2x+25) = 180.
Question 2 · Parallel Lines & Angles
What is the value of x?
Ax = 24
Bx = 28
Cx = 32
Dx = 36
Correct! x = 28
Co-interior angles sum to 180°:
(3x+15) + (2x+25) = 180
5x + 40 = 180
5x = 140
x = 28 ✓
Check: 3(28)+15=99° and 2(28)+25=81° · 99+81=180 ✓

5x+40=180 → 5x=140 → x=28
Problem
A garden is shaped like a trapezoid with parallel sides of 12 m and 20 m, and a height of 8 m. A circular fountain with radius 2 m is placed in the center of the garden.
Find the trapezoid area using A = ½(b₁+b₂)h, then subtract the circular fountain area A = πr². The planted area is the difference.
Question 3 · Composite Area
What is the area of the garden excluding the fountain? (Use π ≈ 3.14)
Aapproximately 115.4 m²
Bapproximately 128 m²
Capproximately 132 m²
Dapproximately 143.4 m²
Correct! ≈ 115.4 m²
Trapezoid: A = ½(12+20)×8 = ½(32)(8) = 128 m²
Fountain: A = π(2)² = 4π ≈ 12.57 m²
Garden area = 128 − 12.57 ≈ 115.4 m² ✓
Not quite. Trapezoid area = ½(b₁+b₂)h = ½(12+20)(8) = 128 m². Fountain area = π(2)² ≈ 12.57 m². Subtract.
128 − 12.57 ≈ 115.4 m²
