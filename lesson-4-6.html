Triangles & Pythagorean Theorem
Master the Pythagorean theorem, the two special right triangles, similar triangles, and the triangle inequality — the geometry tools the SAT uses most frequently.
13 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
The Pythagorean theorem and common triples
a² + b² = c²
where c is the hypotenuse (side opposite the right angle) and a, b are the two legs
The most important Pythagorean triples to memorize — they appear constantly on the SAT and save calculation time:
3-4-5
3, 4, 5
6, 8, 10
9, 12, 15
5, 12, 13
Multiples also work. Check: is the ratio of legs 3:4?
45-45-90
legs: x, x
hypotenuse: x√2
Isosceles right triangle. Half a square. Hypotenuse = leg × √2.
30-60-90
short leg: x
long leg: x√3
hypotenuse: 2x
Half an equilateral triangle. Hypotenuse is always double the short leg.
Similar triangles
Two triangles are similar if their angles are equal. Similar triangles have proportional sides — the ratio of any pair of corresponding sides is the same (the scale factor).
Key similarity conditions
AA (Angle-Angle): Two pairs of equal angles → triangles are similar.
Parallel line in triangle: A line parallel to one side creates a smaller similar triangle at the top.

Setting up proportions: If △ABC ~ △DEF, then:
AB/DE = BC/EF = AC/DF = scale factor k

Area ratio: If side lengths are in ratio k:1, then areas are in ratio k²:1.
Worked example — similar triangles with parallel lines
A 6-foot person casts a 4-foot shadow. At the same time, a tree casts a 22-foot shadow. How tall is the tree?

Person/shadow = tree/shadow: 6/4 = h/22
4h = 132 → h = 33 feet
SAT strategy tips
Recognize Pythagorean triples instantly
Before computing a² + b², check if the sides are a multiple of 3-4-5 or 5-12-13. If legs are 9 and 12: that's 3×(3-4-5) → hypotenuse = 15. This saves 30+ seconds per problem.
30-60-90: hypotenuse is always 2× short leg
If you know the hypotenuse, the short leg is half of it. The long leg is short leg × √3. Never mix these up — the short leg is opposite the 30° angle, the long leg is opposite the 60° angle.
Similar triangles: set up ratios carefully
When setting up proportions, match corresponding sides — the sides opposite equal angles. Label the triangles with their angle measures first, then pair up the sides that face the same angle.
Triangle inequality: sum of any two sides > third
In any triangle, the sum of any two sides must be greater than the third side. The SAT uses this to test whether a given set of measurements can form a valid triangle.

Problem
Two similar triangles have corresponding sides in the ratio 3:5. The area of the smaller triangle is 27 cm².
If sides are in ratio k:1, areas are in ratio k²:1. Here k = 5/3. Area ratio = (5/3)² = 25/9. Larger area = 27 × (25/9).
