Volume & 3D Geometry
Master volume formulas for all 3D shapes on the SAT, learn to work with cross-sections, and solve density and rate-of-fill problems that use volume in context.
12 min read3 practice questions+20 pts on completion
Volume formulas — all shapes on the SAT
Rectangular prism (box)
V = lwh
l = length, w = width, h = height. Surface area = 2(lw + lh + wh).
Cylinder
V = πr²h
r = radius of circular base, h = height. Same as circle area × height.
Cone
V = ⅓πr²h
Exactly ⅓ of the cylinder with same base and height. h = perpendicular height.
Sphere
V = (4/3)πr³
Surface area = 4πr². Note: volume uses r cubed, surface area uses r squared.
Pyramid
V = ⅓ × base area × h
Any pyramid: ⅓ of the prism with same base. Base area depends on shape of base.
Density relationship
Density = mass/volume
mass = density × volume
Common SAT application: find mass given density and dimensions, or find a dimension given mass and density.
Worked example — volume and density
A cylindrical tank has radius 3 m and height 8 m. Water (density 1,000 kg/m³) fills it at 2 m³/minute. How long to fill it completely?

Volume = π(3)²(8) = 72π ≈ 226.2 m³
Time = Volume ÷ Rate = 226.2 ÷ 2 ≈ 113.1 minutes
SAT strategy tips
Cone and pyramid: always multiply by ⅓
Both cones and pyramids have volume = ⅓ × base × height. The ⅓ is the most commonly forgotten factor. A cone is exactly ⅓ of its surrounding cylinder; a pyramid is ⅓ of its surrounding prism.
Scale factor for similar 3D shapes
If linear dimensions scale by factor k, volume scales by k³. If a sphere's radius doubles, its volume is 2³ = 8 times larger. The SAT tests this frequently in "how many times greater" questions.
Cross-section problems
A cross-section is the 2D shape you see when slicing through a 3D solid. A vertical slice through a cylinder = rectangle. A horizontal slice = circle. Knowing cross-sections helps with volume calculations.
Units matter — watch cubic vs. square
Volume is always in cubic units (cm³, m³). Surface area is in square units (cm², m²). If you get a volume answer in cm² — you made an error somewhere. Always check that your answer has the right unit type.
Practice questions
Problem
A cone has a radius of 6 cm and a height of 10 cm.
V = ⅓πr²h. Don't forget the ⅓! Substitute r = 6 and h = 10.
Question 1 · Cone Volume
What is the volume of the cone? (Leave in terms of π.)
A360π cm³
B120π cm³
C240π cm³
D180π cm³
Correct! 120π cm³
V = ⅓πr²h = ⅓ × π × 36 × 10
= ⅓ × 360π = 120π cm³ ✓

Option A (360π) is the CYLINDER volume — missing the ⅓.
Option D (180π) uses ½ instead of ⅓.

Problem
A large sphere has radius 6 cm. A small sphere has radius 2 cm.
Volume scales as the cube of the linear dimension. Scale factor = 6/2 = 3. Volume ratio = 3³ = 27. No need to compute each volume separately.
Question 2 · Volume Scaling
How many times greater is the volume of the large sphere than the small sphere?
A3 times
B9 times
C27 times
D216 times
Correct! 27 times
Linear scale factor k = 6/2 = 3
Volume ratio = k³ = 3³ = 27

Verify: V_large = (4/3)π(6)³ = 288π
V_small = (4/3)π(2)³ = (32/3)π
Ratio = 288π ÷ (32/3)π = 288 × 3/32 = 27 ✓

3³ = 27 times larger
Problem
A rectangular aquarium is 80 cm long, 40 cm wide, and 50 cm tall. It is currently filled to 60% capacity. Water is added at a rate of 2,000 cm³ per minute.
Total volume = 80×40×50 = 160,000 cm³. Current water = 60% × 160,000 = 96,000 cm³. Remaining = 40% × 160,000 = 64,000 cm³. Time = remaining ÷ rate.
Question 3 · Volume Application
How many minutes will it take to fill the aquarium completely?
A32 minutes
B48 minutes
C80 minutes
D64 minutes
Correct! 32 minutes
Total volume = 80×40×50 = 160,000 cm³
Already filled: 60% × 160,000 = 96,000 cm³
Remaining: 40% × 160,000 = 64,000 cm³
Time = 64,000 ÷ 2,000 = 32 minutes ✓
Not quite. Find total volume first (l×w×h). Then 40% remains to be filled. Divide remaining volume by the fill rate.
160,000 × 0.40 = 64,000 cm³ · 64,000 ÷ 2,000 = 32 min
